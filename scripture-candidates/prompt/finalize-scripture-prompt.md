# Primary Goal

Each day should help the user meaningfully engage Scripture and grow in
relationship with:

- God the Father
- Jesus Christ
- the Holy Spirit

The devotional material should encourage users not merely to read Christian
content but to respond faithfully to what Scripture teaches.

The content should help users:

- understand the passage
- understand what it reveals about God
- recognize what it reveals about themselves
- trust God more deeply
- respond through prayer
- examine their life honestly
- obey where Scripture calls for obedience
- receive grace where Scripture offers grace
- grow in their relationship with Jesus
- carry the truth of the passage into ordinary life

77Faithful is explicitly and unapologetically Christian.

Write from within broadly orthodox historic Christianity.

It is appropriate to speak naturally and directly about:

- God
- Jesus Christ
- the Holy Spirit
- Scripture
- grace
- faith
- sin
- repentance
- forgiveness
- obedience
- holiness
- salvation
- the cross
- resurrection
- prayer
- worship
- love
- service
- generosity
- Christian community
- mission
- perseverance

Do not turn Christianity into:

- generic spirituality
- positive thinking
- self-help
- productivity
- moral achievement
- vague inspiration

Do not imply that completing spiritual practices earns God's favor.

Christian disciplines should be presented as ways of intentionally responding to
God's grace, seeking Him, knowing Him, obeying Him, and ordering life around

# Contextual Integrity

Before writing anything for a passage, analyze its biblical context.

Consider:

- the speaker
- the audience
- the surrounding verses
- the argument or narrative
- the book's broader message
- relevant historical context
- whether statements are descriptive or prescriptive
- whether promises are universal or situation-specific
- how the passage relates to Christ and the wider biblical story

Do not proof-text.

Do not turn a passage into a lesson it does not actually teach.

If a popular interpretation of a verse ignores its context, follow the context
rather than the popular interpretation.

---

# Denominational Restraint

Remain firmly Christian without unnecessarily taking sides on secondary
denominational disputes.

Where faithful Christians hold multiple orthodox interpretations, avoid
declaring one disputed position as unquestionably correct unless the text
requires it.

This includes topics such as:

- baptismal particulars
- charismatic gifts
- predestination and free will systems
- communion particulars
- church government
- end-times systems
- other secondary doctrinal disputes

Do not become vague about central Christian doctrine merely to avoid
denominational disagreement.

The seven days should function as a coherent spiritual progression.

Before drafting, determine the strongest order for the seven supplied passages.

Arrange them so the week develops naturally.

A strong progression may move through:

- foundation
- understanding

Avoid repeating the same lesson seven times.

# Required Content for Every Day

Create all of the following for each of the seven passages.

## 1. Scripture

Include:

- day number
- passage reference
- Bible translation
- exact Scripture text

Do not modify supplied Scripture text.

---

## 2. Daily Focus

Create a concise title or focus for the day.

Usually approximately **2–6 words**.

Examples of the level of specificity desired:

- Remain in Christ
- Listen Before Acting
- Pray With Persistence
- Renew Your Mind
- Secure in Christ
- Love Through Action
- Serve Without Recognition

Do not use vague motivational headings.

---

## 3. Devotional Reflection

Write a short devotional reflection based directly on the passage.

Target approximately **120–180 words**, but use judgment.

It may be somewhat shorter when the Scripture is simple and direct or somewhat
longer when additional biblical context is necessary.

Do not pad content to reach a word count.

The devotional should generally accomplish four things naturally:

1. explain what the passage is communicating
2. connect it to the week's theme
3. help the reader examine or understand their own life
4. direct the reader toward a faithful response to God

Do not mechanically structure the devotional as four labeled sections.

It should read as one coherent reflection.

### Tone

The tone should be:

- warm
- grounded
- thoughtful
- encouraging
- biblically serious
- personal without pretending to know the reader's circumstances
- accessible to Christians at different stages of maturity

Avoid:

- sermons written at the reader
- academic commentary
- shallow inspiration
- excessive sentimentality
- guilt manipulation
- clichés
- exaggerated promises
- repetitive Christian filler
- unnecessarily complex theological terminology

Be willing to challenge the reader when the passage calls for it.

Be equally willing to emphasize grace, assurance, rest, hope, forgiveness, or
encouragement when the passage calls for those things.

---

## 4. Prayer Prompt

Write a focused prayer prompt directly connected to both:

- the Scripture passage
- the week's theme

Usually use **one sentence**.

Use two sentences only when that genuinely improves clarity.

The prompt should tell the reader what they might bring before God without
scripting the entire prayer.

Example quality level:

> Ask God to show you where you are trying to produce spiritual growth apart
> from Christ, and ask Him to teach you to depend on Jesus more fully today.

Avoid generic prompts such as:

> Pray about this verse.

---

## 5. Written Prayer

Write an actual prayer the user can pray.

Use **first-person singular** language such as:

- I
- me
- my

Address God naturally.

Depending on the passage, it is appropriate to address:

- Father
- God
- Lord
- Jesus
- Lord Jesus

Do not force every prayer to begin or end identically.

The prayer must emerge from the Scripture rather than merely repeat the
devotional.

It may include, where appropriate:

- praise
- thanksgiving
- confession
- repentance
- dependence
- requests for wisdom
- requests for strength
- requests for transformation
- requests for love
- requests for courage
- surrender
- trust
- worship

Use whatever length is necessary to pray meaningfully.

A typical prayer will probably be around **70–130 words**, but this is not a
hard limit.

Do not make prayers long merely to sound spiritual.

Do not write theatrical or excessively poetic prayers.

Write something a real Christian could sincerely pray.

---

## 6. Reflection Question

Write **one strong reflection question**.

The question should require personal thought rather than factual recall.

It should connect the Scripture to the reader's relationship with God and their
actual life.

Strong example:

> Where are you relying on your own effort to produce something that Jesus is
> asking you to receive through deeper dependence on Him?

Weak examples:

> What did this verse say?

> How does this verse make you feel?

Avoid questions whose obvious expected answer is merely "yes," "no," "pray
more," or "trust God."

---

# Relationship Between the Fields

The fields should reinforce one another without saying the same thing
repeatedly.

For example:

- the devotional explains and applies the passage
- the prayer prompt identifies what to bring before God
- the prayer helps the reader actually pray
- the reflection question helps the reader personally examine and respond

Do not paraphrase the same sentence four different ways.

Each field should perform a distinct function.

---

# Writing Quality

Everything except the Scripture itself must be original writing.

Avoid formulaic repetition between days.

Do not begin every devotional with:

> In this passage...

Do not begin every prayer with the same phrase.

Do not end every prayer with the same generic request.

Do not repeatedly use phrases such as:

- in today's busy world
- in our daily lives
- this powerful reminder
- this beautiful passage
- we are reminded
- lean into
- journey
- season of life
- live this out

Use ordinary, clear Christian language.

Prioritize substance over decorative language.

- the devotional is substantive

### Prayer prompts

- every prompt is specific
- every prompt relates directly to the passage
- every prompt relates to the weekly theme

### Written prayers

- every prayer is first-person singular
- every prayer could realistically be prayed by a user
- prayers are not repetitive
- prayers are biblically consistent
- prayers do more than summarize the devotional

### Reflection questions

- every question requires meaningful thought
- questions are personally applicable
- questions remain grounded in Scripture
- questions are not repetitive
- questions are not superficial
