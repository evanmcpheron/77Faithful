# 06 · Scripture and Daily Content

[Documentation guide](README.md)

**Authority:** This document owns the participant-facing formation content, reading experience, translation expectations, and editorial boundaries.

## Scripture is the primary daily content

Every day has an assigned Scripture reading connected to its weekly theme. The full assigned passage must be readable inside the app in an available selected translation. Showing only a reference, a paraphrased devotional, or a link to another resource does not fulfill the in-app reading requirement.

A participant may nevertheless prefer a printed Bible or another Bible resource. The reference remains easy to find, and Scripture completion can be marked without opening the in-app reader.

V1 is a guided reading experience, not a full Bible-study application. It does not require whole-Bible navigation, passage search, annotation, highlighting, cross-reference tools, or audio recordings.

## What every day contains

| Content element                       | Purpose                                                     | Participant obligation                                                                |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Day number, title, and theme          | Explain where the person is and what the day explores.      | Information only.                                                                     |
| Assigned Scripture reference and text | Provide the primary biblical reading.                       | Engaging with the reading is one foundational practice.                               |
| Short devotional                      | Give useful context and invite a grounded response.         | Available to read; not a separate practice or required proof of Scripture completion. |
| Focused prayer prompt                 | Help the person begin prayer connected to the passage.      | Prayer is a foundational practice; using the exact prompt is optional.                |
| Primary reflection question           | Encourage honest consideration of Scripture and daily life. | Reflection is a foundational practice; written answers are optional.                  |
| Intention invitation                  | Help the person identify a concrete response for the day.   | Optional; never a sixth practice.                                                     |
| Five assigned practice controls       | Record the person's declared participation.                 | Completion remains manual and individual to each practice.                            |

## Assigned readings

Each day normally has one primary passage. A short supporting passage may be included when necessary for context or a clear connection; additional references must not make the reading needlessly fragmented. All assigned portions together are one Scripture practice.

The reading should be a coherent unit rather than a verse isolated solely because it contains a theme word. Editors consider context, the passage's original setting, and its relationship to the week's topic. A short reading is not automatically better if it obscures meaning.

The app displays the actual assigned references and their full text. It must not silently shorten a passage or display a different passage because the chosen translation is inconvenient.

A single V1 course assigns the same daily reading references, devotional, and prompts to participants on the same numbered journey day. A person on Day 10 and another person on Day 30 do not receive the same daily material just because the real-world date matches.

## Bible translation choice

The participant chooses from the translations actually approved and available in the product. The selector shows the full translation name and its abbreviation. NASB must be identified specifically as **NASB 2020** for this intended catalog; another NASB edition must not be substituted under that label.

### Choosing and changing translations

The participant makes an explicit selection during setup. The app does not silently assign a translation based on denomination, perceived spiritual maturity, or payment.

A translation can be changed at any time through the reading page or settings when the replacement can be made available. The new preference affects subsequent reading displays, including viewing historical days. It does not change the assigned references, reset practice completion, or rewrite the participant's previously saved words.

The app must identify the translation currently displayed. It must not mix passages from several translations under one label. Where editions arrange verse numbers differently, the reader must preserve the intended reading honestly rather than inventing omitted text or hiding the difference.

When a different translation requires an internet connection before it can be read without one, the app explains that and keeps the current readable choice available. A failed change must not strand the participant with an empty reader.

## Reading presentation

The Scripture reading should have a clear title and reference, visibly identified translation, readable paragraphs, and appropriate verse numbering. Text size and appearance controls should make longer passages comfortable to read without compromising other accessibility settings.

Scripture text, publisher-required acknowledgments, and the app's own devotional must remain distinguishable. A reader must be able to tell which words are Scripture and which are commentary.

The reader provides a clear path back to the day and a distinct **Mark Scripture complete** action. Navigation, scrolling, time spent on the page, and switching translations do not mark completion automatically.

The devotional must not be placed in a way that makes people believe they read the assigned Scripture when they only read the commentary.

## The daily devotional

V1 includes a short, original devotional for each of the 77 days. The editorial target is approximately **150–250 words**, adjusted when necessary for clarity. This is a writing guideline, not a participant reading quota.

A devotional should explain one central truth of the passage, provide enough context to avoid distortion, and offer a concrete but noncoercive application. It should not turn every passage into generic self-improvement advice.

The devotional must not claim that completing a task will force a blessing, resolve a medical problem, guarantee an answered prayer, or establish the reader's spiritual condition. It must not impersonate a personalized message from God.

The app does not require the participant to read or acknowledge the devotional before marking Scripture complete. Someone reading their personal Bible remains a full participant.

## Prayer prompts

Each day includes a focused prompt, normally one or two short sentences. It may invite thanksgiving, confession, trust, intercession, praise, lament, or another biblically appropriate response connected to the reading.

Prompts should be usable by someone unfamiliar with elaborate prayer language. They should invite personal prayer rather than prescribe a formula or promise a particular outcome.

V1 does not save a separate prayer-entry field. A person who voluntarily mentions prayer in their private daily reflection is not creating a publicly shareable prayer request.

## Intention and reflection

### Intention

The day offers an optional invitation to identify a response such as a concrete act of love, a situation in which to practice trust, or something to remember during the day. It is available at any time, not only before a prescribed morning hour.

The participant may act on the invitation without writing or may save a private short intention. There is no minimum length and no independent intention-complete checkbox. The intention must not become an additional assignment that every participant must fulfill to complete the day.

### Reflection

The main reflection question connects the reading with the person's actual experience. The usual framing invites reflection toward the end of the day, but someone may reflect earlier or revisit a historical day.

There is one primary free-form daily reflection field. There are no required ratings for faith, mood, sincerity, obedience, or spiritual health. V1 does not need a structured survey or multiple compulsory questions.

The writing is private. Saving, editing, or deleting it is separate from marking the Reflect practice complete.

## Weekly introductions and reviews

All eleven theme names and short overview descriptions can be viewed from the beginning. A week's full introduction becomes available when that week begins; earlier introductions remain available. Future daily readings and full weekly introductions are not previewed before their relevant period.

A weekly introduction briefly explains the theme, its connection to the journey so far, and what the coming seven days invite the participant to explore. The editorial target is approximately **100–180 words**.

On the seventh day of each week, the normal reflection question also invites a look back at the week's theme. This replaces that day's ordinary reflection wording; it is not a second mandatory reflection or a sixth practice.

Day 77 connects perseverance with reviewing the whole journey and continuing afterward. A participant does not need to complete a final survey to finish the period.

## Content stability and corrections

The formation course a person begins should remain coherent throughout their journey. Routine future editorial revisions should not silently replace historical readings, prompts, and devotionals that gave context to the person's writing.

Necessary factual, theological, safety, or rights-related corrections may still be made. Material corrections should be clearly identified, preserve the person's writing, and explain any reading that is no longer available. The app must not continue displaying unauthorized or harmful content merely to preserve the appearance of an unchanged course.

Changing a translation preference is different from changing the course. It changes the text edition displayed, not the assigned day's purpose or references.

## Availability without internet

An established journey should make its guidance and assigned Scripture available on a phone once preparation is complete, as defined in Document 12. The released translation catalog must support the specific reading experience the app promises.

If a person encounters an unavailable reading before preparation has completed or on a newly signed-in phone, the page shows the reference, an accurate explanation, and the option to use their own Bible. Manual completion remains available where the day itself is available. An error message is not a substitute for meeting the release's normal in-app reading requirement.

## Editorial readiness

The course is not ready merely because 77 titles and references exist. Every day requires reviewed Scripture selection, authorized text, a devotional, a prayer prompt, a reflection question, and an intention invitation. Every week requires a reviewed introduction.

The [77-day outline](07-eleven-week-formation-plan.md) and [illustrative day](18-illustrative-day.md) guide that work. They are not a complete, approved publishing manuscript.
