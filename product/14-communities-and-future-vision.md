# 14 · Communities and Future Vision

[Documentation guide](README.md)

**Everything in this document is outside V1 unless it restates a permanent principle.** Private communities are a committed future direction. Other ideas are explicitly identified as possibilities, not promises or assigned release dates.

## Why communities belong in the vision

77Faithful is intended eventually to support people practicing faith together, not only alone. Friends, families, Bible studies, small groups, and churches should be able to organize private communities around encouragement, shared Scripture, prayer, and accountability.

A community is not a public audience to impress. Its purpose is to support real relationships and faithful participation while respecting the personal nature of each member's journey.

The first release remains a complete personal app. It must not include unfinished group screens, invitations that do nothing, or public sharing merely because communities are planned.

## Committed future capabilities

| Capability                   | Intended participant benefit                                                                       | Boundary                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Private community creation   | Let an organizer create a space for known participants.                                            | No payment, paid size tier, or public popularity requirement.                                   |
| Invitation-based membership  | Let people deliberately join the appropriate community.                                            | Do not make journals visible through an invitation or import a person's contacts automatically. |
| Coordinated group journeys   | Let participants follow the same day, theme, assigned passages, and devotional direction together. | Each participant still has a personal record and no more than one active journey.               |
| Shared prayer requests       | Let members voluntarily ask their community for prayer.                                            | Separate shared content from the person's private prayer or reflection.                         |
| Encouragement and discussion | Support thoughtful responses to readings, weekly themes, and shared requests.                      | No competitive rankings or compulsory posting.                                                  |
| Group progress               | Give an encouraging view of shared participation.                                                  | Never expose detailed missed practices or private writing by default.                           |
| Organizer announcements      | Communicate group plans and relevant reminders.                                                    | No advertising, sponsored content, coercive spiritual supervision, or donation pressure.        |
| Member safety controls       | Let people report content, control unwanted interactions, and leave a group.                       | Organizers do not receive unrestricted access to personal information.                          |

These capabilities form a product direction. They do not imply that every possible social feature is needed in the first community release.

## What joining a community should mean

The person receives an invitation that identifies the community and its organizer. Before joining, they can understand the group's purpose, membership visibility, expected participation, and any shared journey schedule.

Joining is explicit and cancelable before acceptance. An invitation must not enroll someone automatically, start a personal journey without consent, or expose their previous history.

Members may use an appropriate display name; a legal name is not inherently required. Contact email and private account information remain hidden from ordinary group views.

Membership in several private communities may be allowed later, but it must not create several simultaneous active journeys. The rule of one active journey per person remains the default for the defined product.

## Shared journey schedule

A coordinated group journey uses one shared course, start date, journey time zone, day numbering, and weekly progression. Members may choose different available Bible translations while reading the same assigned references.

Each person keeps the three foundational practices and chooses their own two additional practices. A group organizer cannot remove Scripture, prayer, or reflection or require the whole group to adopt an unsuitable physical or financial practice.

Coordinating a future group may require agreeing on a start date beforehand. That is future group behavior, not a reason to add scheduled personal starts to V1.

### Existing active journeys

A group invitation must not silently replace, end, merge, or renumber a person's existing active journey. The person should be able to remain in the community without abandoning their personal journey.

To join a new coordinated journey, they must be eligible under the one-active-journey rule and deliberately accept the new commitment. They can wait for a later group journey rather than being pressured to erase or end their current experience.

### Initial future enrollment default

For an initial community journey experience, enrollment should occur before the shared journey starts. A person who joins the community after that journey begins can still participate in appropriate community discussion and plan for the next shared journey, but should not receive a falsely backdated personal record.

More flexible late enrollment is a separate future product decision. It must resolve how a full 77-day personal experience relates to the group's already elapsed days rather than quietly weakening the duration or historical rules.

## Community progress without competition

The group can always discuss the shared current theme and schedule. Individual participation information is different: it should be shared only through clear member choices.

The initial future default is that membership does not expose a member's exact practice markers, missed days, longest streak, starting motivation, or private writing. A member may deliberately offer a high-level progress update or enable an appropriately explained progress-sharing option.

Aggregate summaries must explain whose shared information they include. They must not pretend to represent all members if some have chosen not to contribute their progress. Small-group numbers can also reveal individuals indirectly; summaries should be omitted or limited when they would defeat a member's choice not to disclose.

There is no ordered list of most faithful members, completion ranking, attendance-style discipline report, or public identification of who missed prayer. A church or small-group leader cannot purchase greater visibility.

A member with an incomplete day remains welcome. Community reminders must not pressure them to fabricate a complete record for the sake of the group.

## Prayer requests and encouragement

A shared prayer request is a deliberate community post, not an automatically copied private journal entry. The member sees the destination and intended audience before posting.

The member should be able to edit or remove their request, decide whether it is still current, and choose what personal detail to include. Naming someone else or describing a sensitive situation requires care; the product should encourage consent and restraint.

Replies and simple acknowledgments may support prayer and encouragement. They must not become popularity competitions or produce public reaction rankings. Posting is never a daily practice requirement, and receiving no response does not mark a spiritual failure.

An eventual option to identify a request as answered should belong to the author and use humble language. It must not imply that all unanswered requests are caused by insufficient faith or participation.

## Private reflections remain separate

If a future member chooses to share a reflection, the product should create a clearly identified shared copy with an explicit audience. The original private reflection remains private.

The sharing confirmation makes clear what text will be visible. Later editing the private original does not automatically publish those new words. Deleting the shared copy does not delete the private original, and deleting the private original does not silently claim to withdraw an already shared copy.

A member can remove the shared copy from the app, subject to truthful policy disclosures. They must understand that another member may already have copied or remembered it; universal recall of shared information is not promised.

Joining another group never extends the audience of an existing shared item automatically.

## Organizer responsibilities and limits

Organizers may describe the community, invite members, coordinate a shared journey, post appropriate announcements, and manage membership under published rules.

They do not own members' personal accounts, journeys, motivations, or journals. Removing a person from a group must not delete that person's private history. Leaving a group must not erase the member's own reached-day record.

If a shared journey continues after a person leaves, that person should be able to continue the same active personal journey privately, retaining its existing schedule. Leaving the group does not create a fresh Day 1 or disclose the reason for leaving.

Transferring organizer responsibility or closing a community must have a deliberate process. Neither action grants a new organizer access to previously private material.

## Safety and moderation are prerequisites

Communities must not launch without clear conduct rules, ways to report harmful content, appropriate moderation responsibility, member removal rules, and controls for unwanted interaction.

The product should address harassment, coercive religious pressure, financial solicitation, exposure of another person's private information, unsafe medical claims, and abuse of spiritual authority. A report about an organizer must have a path that does not depend solely on that organizer reviewing their own conduct.

Moderation information itself is not a public group feed. Leaving, reporting, or declining to share must not generate a public accusation that the person is unfaithful.

Future youth participation requires explicit safeguarding decisions before communities accept that use. Unrestricted private messaging and live chat are not assumed requirements.

## Possible later additions — not committed deliveries

| Idea                                          | What would need to remain true                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Scheduled personal journey starts             | Dates and pre-start behavior must be clear; V1 remains immediate-start only.                           |
| Alternative courses or varied repeat journeys | Scripture remains central; a group on one shared journey follows the same selected course.             |
| Journal search or bookmarks                   | Privacy is preserved; these support review rather than spiritual scoring.                              |
| Export or a personal journey keepsake         | The participant controls the material included; Scripture reuse must respect the relevant permissions. |
| Audio reading or additional languages         | Accurate content, accessibility, and approved use remain prerequisites.                                |
| Personal prayer journal                       | It stays distinct from shared community prayer requests.                                               |
| Family or youth account arrangements          | Consent, safeguarding, and the child's own privacy are defined explicitly before inclusion.            |

## Optional support for 77Faithful

A donation option is a possible later addition, not V1. It exists only to let a person voluntarily support an app that remains entirely free.

A tasteful location would be a single **Support 77Faithful** item in About or Settings. It should not appear as an interruption during Scripture, prayer, reflection, a missed-day return, or the completion summary.

No donation unlocks content, translations, reminders, history, communities, church tools, access limits, priority spiritual care, recognition, or a badge. No advertisement appears for people who do not give. There are no public donor rankings.

The wording should explain what support funds without implying that God requires payment, that giving earns favor, or that gratitude must be expressed financially. Generosity as a daily practice must never be linked to a requirement to donate to the app.

Before adding a support option, review the actual fundraising disclosures and any affected Scripture-use permissions. Do not assume that an optional donation arrangement automatically has the same permission terms as an app without it.

## Permanent limits continue to apply

Future scope does not weaken the free-access promise, privacy of personal writing, biblical editorial responsibility, no-reset philosophy, one-active-journey principle, or prohibition on competition and paid spiritual access.
