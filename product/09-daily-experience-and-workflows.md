# 09 · Daily Experience and Workflows

[Documentation guide](README.md)

This document follows people through the product in ordinary language. The specialist documents own the underlying product rules; these workflows show how those rules fit together.

## Workflow 1 · First visit through Day 1

**Starting situation:** The person has never used 77Faithful.

They open Login, with account-entry choices and access to About & Help and Weekly Themes. Concise product context and the available introduction explain that the app is a free, biblically based, 77-day journey with five daily practices. They can understand the purpose and view the broad eleven-week progression before creating an account or committing; no separate Welcome destination is required.

They create or access a personal account and confirm their email. They learn the three foundational practices, choose two distinct additional practices, and select an available Bible translation. They may record why they are beginning and may choose reminder times, but neither writing nor reminders are required.

The final review shows their five practices, today's start date, the Day 77 date, and the journey time zone. Selecting **Start my journey** begins the period only after the start is confirmed.

Today then shows Day 1. They can read the first week's introduction, see the assigned passage, read the Scripture, and begin the five practices. Completing setup has not automatically marked any practice complete.

**Successful outcome:** The person knows what they are doing, why they are doing it, and that an imperfect day will not force a restart.

## Workflow 2 · Begin a normal day with an intention

**Starting situation:** The participant has an active journey.

They open Today and immediately see **Day 18 of 77**, the current date in the journey time zone, the weekly theme, the assigned reading, and the five-practice status. The actual number and date come from their own journey.

An optional invitation asks how they might respond today. They can consider it without writing or save a short private intention. Skipping this invitation does not create an incomplete sixth item.

They enter Scripture, read the passage in their chosen translation, and may read the devotional. They mark Scripture complete when ready, then return to Today. They open Prayer, use the prompt, pray, and mark Pray complete.

The day now shows **2 of 5 practices complete**. The remaining practices remain visible without a warning that the person is behind.

**Successful outcome:** The app helps the person begin with Scripture and prayer while allowing the day's practices to unfold in a realistic order.

## Workflow 3 · Read in a personal Bible

**Starting situation:** The participant prefers a printed Bible.

Today shows the assigned reference clearly. The person reads that passage in their own Bible, reflects on it, and later opens the app to mark Scripture complete.

The app does not demand that they open the in-app reading, scroll through it, or match the translation used outside the app. The assigned passage remains the common reading; their preferred reading location does not exclude them from participation.

They can still use the app's devotional, prayer prompt, and reflection question.

**Successful outcome:** The app supports Scripture engagement rather than treating screen activity as proof.

## Workflow 4 · Complete practices throughout the day

**Starting situation:** The participant has completed Scripture and prayer and selected Movement and Serve or Encourage.

They carry out appropriate movement and offer genuine encouragement to someone. They mark each practice complete from Today using clearly labeled controls. Opening a practice's title instead would display its purpose and examples.

They do not enter minutes, distances, photographs, the recipient's name, or evidence that the encouragement was received favorably.

Later they return for reflection. The app resumes the same day's actual status and optional saved intention rather than creating another daily record.

**Successful outcome:** Everyday action is easy to record without turning the experience into surveillance or a performance contest.

## Workflow 5 · Reflect without writing

**Starting situation:** The participant has reached the end of a busy day and does not want to type.

They open Reflect, read the day's question, and consider their response privately. They can see their saved intention, if they wrote one, as context.

They mark Reflect complete without entering text. The app accepts this normally. The Reflections collection does not invent a journal entry because a completion marker exists.

If all five practices are marked complete, the day shows **All five practices complete**. There is no demand for an explanation, minimum word count, or extra final action.

**Successful outcome:** Reflection is encouraged without equating it with typing.

## Workflow 6 · Write and later edit a reflection

**Starting situation:** The participant wants a private record of what they noticed.

They open Reflect, write freely, and save. The screen distinguishes a saved entry from an unsaved draft and from whether the Reflect practice is marked complete.

They may mark the practice separately. Later they find the day through Reflections or Journey, correct their writing, and save again. The entry remains associated with its original journey day and date, with an update date when appropriate.

Deleting the written entry removes the text after confirmation. It does not automatically change the day's practice markers. Deleting a saved intention likewise does not create an incomplete practice.

**Successful outcome:** Personal writing is editable and private without confusing it with the participation checklist.

## Workflow 7 · Leave a day partly complete

**Starting situation:** The participant reaches the end of a day with three completed practices.

The day remains accurately recorded as **3 of 5 practices complete**. The app does not label the person a failure or require a reason.

At midnight in the journey time zone, the next numbered day becomes Today. The previous day remains available in history. The participant can continue today immediately or voluntarily revisit the older day.

The complete-day streak may change according to the recorded days, but the journey itself continues and all earlier participation remains.

**Successful outcome:** Accountability remains honest without making perfection the entry requirement for tomorrow.

## Workflow 8 · Return after several missed days

**Starting situation:** The participant last opened Day 12 and returns when Day 17 is current.

Today shows Day 17, not Day 13 or a reset Day 1. The message might say: **Continue with today's reading. Your earlier days are still available.**

They can begin Day 17 immediately. Journey shows earlier dates and their actual recorded states. No backlog, apology form, or catch-up sequence blocks the current day.

If they decide to revisit Day 14, it opens as a historical day with its original date and assigned practices. They can update it without changing Day 17.

**Successful outcome:** Returning is straightforward and does not depend on repairing every earlier record.

## Workflow 9 · Update an earlier day

**Starting situation:** The participant remembers that they prayed on Day 9 but did not record it, or wants to revisit Day 9's material later.

They open Journey, choose the correct journey, and select Day 9. The screen clearly says that it is a past day and shows the original date.

They mark Pray complete or make another eligible change. The day carries an **Updated after this day** indication and an update date. The corresponding journey summary changes to reflect the revised record.

Nothing about the current day's practices, course position, or start date changes. The app does not claim that the historical update proves the activity occurred on its originally assigned date.

**Successful outcome:** The record is correctable without presenting retroactive entries as independently verified on-time activity.

## Workflow 10 · Change an optional practice

**Starting situation:** The participant wants to replace Movement with Gratitude on Day 24.

They open My Practices from Settings or the day's practice guidance. They see their current selections and choose a new valid pair, retaining the other practice if desired.

The review says the replacement begins on Day 25 and displays that date. Today's five practices do not change. They confirm while connected, or keep the existing pair if confirmation cannot be completed.

Before Day 25 begins, they may revise or cancel the upcoming change. When the next journey day begins, the new pair appears. Historical days keep their earlier selections.

A health or safety concern never requires the person to continue an unsuitable activity while waiting for tomorrow. They may leave it incomplete; their journey continues.

**Successful outcome:** The product accommodates changing circumstances while keeping an intelligible historical record.

## Workflow 11 · Travel to another time zone

**Starting situation:** A journey began in New York time and the participant travels elsewhere.

The current journey day continues to follow New York time. When the local phone date differs, Today displays an unobtrusive explanation and the journey's date.

The person can still read, pray, record, and reflect normally. Reminder times follow the phone's local clock as described in Document 12, but reminders open the actual current journey day. Changing reminder behavior does not change the journey dates.

**Successful outcome:** Travel does not duplicate days, erase participation, or make the course move backward.

## Workflow 12 · Continue without internet

**Starting situation:** The active journey has already been prepared on this phone, but the phone has no internet connection.

The participant can open the current day's assigned Scripture and guidance, see their practices, write an intention or reflection, and save participation. Closing and reopening the app does not remove changes that were confirmed saved on that phone.

A clear, unobtrusive status explains when changes are saved only on this phone and are not yet available on other signed-in phones. When the connection returns, the app makes those changes available to the account without requiring the person to reenter them.

Starting or ending a journey, changing the optional pair, changing to unavailable reading material, and permanent account deletion require a connection. The app explains this distinction rather than failing silently.

**Successful outcome:** An ordinary disconnected day does not prevent the core formation experience or mislead the participant about the safety of their record.

## Workflow 13 · End a journey early

**Starting situation:** The participant deliberately decides not to continue the current journey.

They open journey details and choose **End this journey**. A confirmation explains that the calendar will stop, existing history will remain, the journey cannot be resumed, and the unused days will not unlock within it.

They may cancel and continue. If they confirm while connected, the journey receives an **Ended early** summary showing the ending date and last reached day.

They can still review and update reached days. A new journey, if started, is separate and begins with its own Day 1 on the new confirmation date.

**Successful outcome:** The choice is respected and recorded accurately without disguising an early ending as full completion.

## Workflow 14 · Reach Day 77 and return afterward

**Starting situation:** The participant reaches the final scheduled day.

Today identifies **Day 77 of 77** and provides the final reading and reflection. It may show a preliminary summary but still treats Day 77 as active until its date ends.

After midnight in the journey time zone, the full period is complete automatically. On the next visit, the participant sees the completed journey with actual participation counts and their starting motivation, if saved.

They can revisit the Day 77 reflection, review earlier entries, or consider what to continue. They are not asked to donate, share publicly, or complete a survey to unlock the ending.

**Successful outcome:** The period ends clearly while communicating that growth continues beyond it.

## Workflow 15 · Begin again without losing history

**Starting situation:** The participant has a completed or early-ended journey and no active one.

They select **Start another journey**, review their two optional practices and translation, optionally record a new motivation, and confirm the new dates.

The new journey starts at Day 1. The earlier journey remains in Journey and its writing remains in Reflections. A journey label accompanies each historical entry so that identical day numbers are not confused.

V1 uses the same course again. The app does not promise a different set of passages merely because this is another attempt.

**Successful outcome:** Repetition supports continued formation without erasing or merging the person's past experience.

## Workflow 16 · Decline reminders

**Starting situation:** The participant does not want reminders, or the phone does not allow them.

They skip reminder setup or turn reminders off. All other functions remain available. The app does not repeatedly interrupt the daily experience to ask for permission again.

Settings explains how to enable reminders later. No notification is used to shame the person for refusing notifications.

**Successful outcome:** Reminders serve the person's choice rather than functioning as a condition of faithfulness.

## Workflow 17 · Sign out on a shared phone

**Starting situation:** The participant is finished using a phone that someone else may use.

They select Sign out. If all saved work is protected for the account, sign-out returns to Login with access to public product information and removes access to private pages on that phone.

If changes exist only on the phone, the app explains which work may be lost. The person may remain signed in to reconnect and protect it, or explicitly discard the unprotected changes and sign out.

The next person does not see the previous participant's writing or receive that person's journey reminders.

**Successful outcome:** Sign-out is understandable, protects privacy, and does not silently sacrifice personal writing.

## Workflow 18 · Request permanent account deletion

**Starting situation:** The participant wants to remove their account and personal record.

They open Account settings and select the clearly labeled deletion option. The explanation distinguishes account deletion from signing out and from ending a journey. It names the information affected and states the actual retention limitations disclosed by the service.

The participant confirms their identity and intention while connected. A canceled attempt changes nothing. A confirmed request gives an accurate status rather than claiming completion before it has occurred.

Once deletion is known on a phone, private pages and reminders must not continue to reveal the deleted account. Immediate removal from another disconnected phone is not promised. The person is told what happens next and how to obtain help with a failed request.

**Successful outcome:** Deletion is available, deliberate, and accurately represented, without an emotional retention appeal or payment requirement.

## Workflow 19 · Participate alongside family or friends in V1

**Starting situation:** Two people want to use the app together before community features exist.

Each person maintains their own account and personal journey. They may agree outside the app to start on the same day and discuss the shared course voluntarily.

The app does not combine their records, grant one person access to the other's journal, or claim they are members of an in-app group. They may read and pray together in ordinary life while recording their own participation.

**Successful outcome:** The personal V1 product supports real relationships without pretending future community functionality already exists.
