# 13 · Privacy, Safety, and Accessibility

[Documentation guide](README.md)

**Authority:** This document owns the participant's privacy controls, personal-information boundaries, safety expectations, and accessibility requirements.

## Privacy in ordinary language

77Faithful contains information about a person's faith, struggles, intentions, relationships, and private thoughts. The product must treat that information with care even when a person voluntarily entered it.

Private writing is not content for a social feed. The app must not assume that signing up, choosing a practice, joining a future group, or receiving help from a church gives other people permission to read it.

### Information and visibility in V1

| Information | Why it exists | Who sees it in the participant experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contact email | Account access and necessary account communication. | The account holder, not other participants. |
| Optional preferred name | Personal address and account recognition. | The account holder; there is no public profile. |
| Journey dates and status | Establish the person's course position and history. | The account holder only. |
| Selected practices and their changes | Describe the five-part commitment for each day. | The account holder only. |
| Practice completion | Maintain an honest personal participation record. | The account holder only. |
| Starting motivation | Revisit the reason for beginning. | The account holder only. |
| Daily intention and reflection | Retain optional personal writing. | The account holder only. |
| Translation and reminder preferences | Support the person's reading and notification choices. | The account holder through the relevant settings. |

There is no in-app sharing in V1. A person can choose independently to discuss their experience or copy their own writing elsewhere, but that is not automatic app sharing or permission for the app to publish it.

## Limits on collection and secondary use

Only request information that serves the defined experience. Do not collect a detailed biography, church attendance, income, diagnoses, denominational identity, or private prayer content simply because it might be interesting later.

Do not sell personal information, use it for advertising, build a spiritual or emotional profile from private writing, train unrelated products on journal entries, or use a participant's faith struggles to solicit money.

Private entries must not be routinely browsed by people operating the app. Any exceptional access genuinely required for support, safety, or legal obligations must be restricted, justified, and described truthfully in the published privacy policy. “Private” must not be exaggerated into a claim that no authorized person could ever access information under any circumstances.

The product must not imply that private reflections are continuously read for pastoral care or emergency intervention. Writing something urgent in the journal is not a request to a monitored support service.

## Participant control of writing

The participant can view, edit, and delete saved daily intentions, daily reflections, and their journey motivation. Deleting a field does not secretly delete the entire day, alter practice completion, or end the journey.

Deletion controls identify the exact information being removed and offer a cancel option. The app must distinguish deleting an entry, ending a journey, signing out, and deleting an account.

When a participant deletes writing while disconnected, its immediate removal on that phone and its removal from the account elsewhere are different statuses. The app must make outstanding removal clear rather than claim every copy has already disappeared.

## Account deletion

Permanent account deletion is available from within account settings without payment or a requirement to contact a group leader. The participant confirms both the account and their intention to proceed.

The explanation covers account information, journeys, practice records, motivations, intentions, and reflections. It states the actual consequences and any limited retention required by the adopted privacy policy. The app must not promise immediate destruction of every backup or copy if that promise cannot be met.

Before release, the owner must publish accurate retention periods, any justified exceptions, how a request is completed, and how someone can seek help if it fails. These documents intentionally do not invent a legal retention period or claim that a privacy policy already exists.

The product shows whether deletion is requested, in progress, unsuccessful, or complete. A failed attempt must not be described as successful, and a canceled attempt must not remove information.

Once deletion is known on a phone, personal pages and journey reminders stop and older saved changes must not recreate the account. Immediate removal from another disconnected phone or from copies the person made outside the app cannot be promised.

## Account and shared-phone privacy

Someone using the same phone after another person signs out must not see that former participant's reflection previews, account details, progress, or personal reminders.

Changing accounts must never merge journeys or transfer pending writing to the newly signed-in person. Account recovery must return the rightful participant to their own records.

A support request must not automatically include a private journal entry. The participant may choose to share a relevant excerpt deliberately, after seeing what will be sent. Reporting a content problem can identify the assigned day without disclosing personal writing.

Reminder previews must remain general. The app should avoid exposing sensitive text through ordinary screen previews where the phone permits protecting them, but must not promise that it can prevent every screenshot, recording, or observation by someone with access to an unlocked phone.

## Safety of the practices

No practice requires a person to injure themselves, ignore appropriate care, jeopardize essential responsibilities, or disregard another person's consent.

**Movement** has no universal fitness target. The app presents adaptable examples and respects physical limitations.

**Intentional Discipline** does not default to food fasting. The app prescribes no fasting duration and never instructs someone to stop necessary food, water, medication, or care. Questions about a person's health suitability belong with an appropriate professional, not a completion checklist.

**Generosity** can involve time, attention, or resources other than money. The app never directs someone to give beyond their means or treats payment to 77Faithful as a practice requirement.

**Service, witness, and family devotion** must respect consent and safety. The app does not pressure contact with strangers, coercive religious conversations, or participation by unwilling household members.

**Forgiveness and community content** must not require unsafe reconciliation, remaining in abuse, disclosing trauma publicly, or deferring to a harmful group leader. Biblical encouragement must not be used to override legitimate personal boundaries.

## Distress, pastoral care, and support

The app may encourage a participant to seek trusted pastoral support, appropriate professional care, or emergency help when relevant. It must not diagnose mental illness, prescribe treatment, promise healing from app use, or claim to replace professional or local support.

Private journal writing is not monitored for emergencies. Help and safety information should explain this clearly. Any emergency contact information published must be appropriate to where it is offered and verified before release; do not publish an invented universal number.

The product does not provide private pastoral messaging, crisis chat, or automated spiritual counseling in V1.

## Young participants and families

The mission welcomes anyone seeking Jesus. The initial experience is designed for independently managed personal accounts, not child-specific profiles, parent-controlled journals, or a school monitoring system.

Before public release, the owner must settle account eligibility, any age restrictions or parental requirements, the regions in which the app will be offered, and the associated safeguarding and privacy disclosures. Do not interpret the broad spiritual invitation as permission to accept independent accounts from every age in every location without review.

Families can read Scripture and practice faith together outside any in-app group feature. An adult's personal account still represents that adult's record; it is not a shared family score or implicit consent to store a child's private journal under the adult's identity.

Any future youth or family-account product requires its own explicit decisions about control, consent, visibility, and safety. A future church or group leader must not automatically gain access to a younger participant's private reflections.

## Accessibility and an approachable experience

Accessibility belongs to V1, not a paid tier or a future enhancement category. The app should work for people who need larger text, assistive reading, higher readability, less precise touch, and a calmer visual experience.

### Reading and comprehension

Use plain language, comfortable text spacing, meaningful headings, and readable passages in both light and dark appearance. Support larger text without hiding actions, cutting off verses, or requiring the participant to reduce the text size to continue.

Explain unfamiliar Christian terms where they matter. Do not assume someone knows how to find an obscure settings symbol or interpret a color-only calendar.

Scripture should remain accurate when text size changes. Verse labels, passage context, and the distinction between Scripture and devotional commentary remain understandable.

### Controls and navigation

Buttons and selectable practices should be comfortably distinguishable and easy to activate. Text labels explain what an action does. Opening guidance and marking completion are distinct actions.

People using assistive reading should hear the practice name, whether it is complete, and the action available. A day grid needs a readable list alternative. States such as complete, future, and unavailable must not rely only on color.

The app must not require precision gestures, complex swipes, long presses, or motion effects for a primary task. A person should have enough time to read and act; no timed response is required for daily participation.

### Writing and errors

Longer text must remain readable while composing a reflection, and the Save action must remain accessible when the phone's keyboard is visible. Errors should explain what happened and how to recover without deleting the person's words.

A person who cannot or does not want to type can still complete Reflect. A person who receives Scripture through an accessible method can still complete Read Scripture.

### Visual character

The visual experience should be modern, calm, cohesive, and approachable for younger and older participants. It should not resemble a children's reward game, a competitive fitness challenge, a dense administrative dashboard, or a collection of unrelated screens.

This is a product quality requirement, not a specification of particular colors, fonts, or drawing methods.

## Future sharing boundaries

Future community membership does not alter the privacy of existing writing. Sharing must name the item, audience, and action explicitly. A group leader's role cannot override that choice.

Private account contact details, other people's contact information, account-recovery information, and private moderation reports must never be exposed through a general “share my journey” action. A participant's own reflection may be voluntarily shared in a future version, but not presumed public because its subject is religious.

The detailed future community boundaries appear in Document 14 and remain outside V1.
