# 08 · Onboarding and Account Experience

[Documentation guide](README.md)

**Authority:** This document owns introduction, account-related participant outcomes, journey setup, and the transition into the experience.

## Before asking for a commitment

Someone opening 77Faithful for the first time should understand what it is before being asked to start a journey. The introduction explains the mission, 77-day duration, five to seven practices, eleven themes, and faithfulness-over-perfection approach in ordinary language.

The signed-out experience begins at Sign In, with access to Create Account, Recover Access, About & Help, and Weekly Themes. Concise product context and access to the introduction remain available before account creation and journey commitment; a separate Welcome destination is not required.

The person may read the overview of the course and the optional-practice descriptions without creating a journey. They should be told that the app is permanently free and that private reflections are not shared with other participants.

The introduction does not require a profession of faith, denominational selection, church membership, financial information, or a promise to complete every practice perfectly.

## Personal accounts in V1

A personal account is required before starting and saving a journey. V1 does not include a guest journey that later needs to be converted or combined with another record.

From the participant's standpoint, the account experience must support account creation, email confirmation, signing in, access recovery, account contact changes, and signing out. The pages explain the steps in plain language and provide a clear way to return when an attempt fails.

The product does not specify the underlying method used to establish account access. Regardless of the chosen sign-in experience, someone must be able to understand which account they are using and recover access without losing or accidentally duplicating their journey.

### Information requested

A contact email address is required for this baseline. It must be confirmed before a journey can start. A preferred name is optional and can be changed later; a legal name is not required.

V1 does not ask for a profile photo, a public biography, denomination, home church, postal address, occupation, income, or detailed religious history. Any age-related eligibility step required for release must be limited to what the adopted safeguarding and privacy policy actually needs; no birth-date collection is assumed merely for personalization.

Consent to unrelated marketing is not a condition of participation. The app does not enroll people in promotional messages as part of their spiritual commitment.

### Unconfirmed and returning accounts

A person who has not finished confirming their email sees how to complete that step, request another confirmation, correct an incorrectly entered address, or return to sign in. The app does not start a journey that they cannot reliably return to.

A returning participant with an active journey goes to Today. Someone with unfinished setup returns to the unfinished step. Someone with only ended journeys sees their history and a clear option to start another.

When the app is still determining whether an existing journey is present, it shows a neutral waiting state rather than pretending the account is empty and encouraging a duplicate start.

## Recommended setup sequence

Journey setup is one guided flow with internal steps, including review and deliberate start. Account access and confirmation use the account experience when needed, rather than a duplicate account form within setup. The sequence below describes the participant's required understanding and choices, not separate destinations for every step.

### Step 1 · Understand the commitment

Explain the three foundational practices and that two to four additional practices will be chosen. State that the period lasts 77 calendar days, not 77 perfect days. Explain that missed days remain in the history and the calendar continues.

Show the eleven-week progression briefly. Do not turn this introduction into eleven compulsory lessons or require the person to read all future content.

### Step 2 · Establish account access

Let the person create or access their account and confirm their email as needed. Keep explanations short and focused on retaining their private journey and returning later.

Account creation and email confirmation do not start Day 1. If the person leaves at this point, no journey days begin passing.

### Step 3 · Choose two to four additional practices

Show the catalog with practical descriptions and suitability boundaries. Keep the three required practices visible so the person understands the complete daily commitment.

Two to four distinct selections are required. The person can change their selections before starting. No written goal or reason is required.

### Step 4 · Choose an available Bible translation

Show full translation names and abbreviations. Offer only choices that are actually available for the promised experience. Explain that the participant may read the same assigned passage in their personal Bible and may change their app translation later.

A desired but unavailable translation must not be shown as an active option. Unavailable offerings are not paid upgrades.

### Step 5 · Optionally record a starting motivation

Offer a single private question such as **What are you hoping to grow in as you spend time with Jesus?**

The field is optional and can be skipped. It is not a vow, a spiritual diagnosis, or a commitment to reach a promised outcome. The participant may revisit, edit, or delete it later. When edited after the journey begins, its update date remains clear; the app does not pretend an edited statement is necessarily the original wording.

The motivation is available from journey details and at the completion summary so the participant can compare their starting hopes with what they experienced. No written motivation is required to reach Day 1.

### Step 6 · Optionally set reminders

Offer a morning reminder and an evening reflection reminder, each independently optional. Let the person choose times or skip the step.

Ask the phone for permission to show reminders only after the participant expresses interest in enabling them. Declining the phone's permission must not block the journey or be treated as a lack of commitment.

Reminders can also be configured later in Settings. They do not need to be enabled before Start becomes available.

### Step 7 · Review and start

Show the daily practices, selected translation, start date, final-day date, and fixed journey time zone. Include a concise statement that days continue after an absence and that the optional practices can be changed for later days.

The action is **Start my journey**. Until that action is confirmed, there is no active journey and no countdown.

If the date changes while the person is reviewing, the review must update and make the actual start date clear before confirmation. It must not silently start on yesterday's displayed date.

If another active journey is discovered, the app takes the person to that journey instead of creating another. Repeatedly selecting Start does not create repeated journeys.

## Entering Day 1

After confirmation, Today shows Day 1, the first week's theme, its introduction, the assigned Scripture, and the daily practices. A brief orientation may point out the reading and optional intention, but the participant should be able to begin immediately.

The app explains when the journey is ready to read and record without internet. Starting the calendar and finishing preparation of the reading material are distinct statuses; the participant must not be misled about either.

No completion marker is preselected because the person finished onboarding. No paid offer, donation request, public sharing prompt, or group invitation interrupts the first day.

## Pausing setup and returning later

People may leave setup without losing completed choices that have been confirmed as saved. When returning to the same account, they resume or review those choices rather than being told a journey already started.

An unstarted setup uses the current date when the person finally selects Start. Earlier setup dates do not become historical journey days.

The participant can also cancel setup. Canceling setup does not delete the account or a previous journey.

## Starting another journey

Returning participants do not need to repeat the full educational introduction. They review their practices and translation, may write a new motivation, and confirm the dates. They may revisit explanatory material when needed.

Existing reminder preferences can be retained, but the review makes clear whether reminders will be active. No reminder is reenabled against a previous choice to disable it.

The new journey remains separate from earlier records and uses the same fixed V1 course.

## Account changes and recovery

Changing a preferred name does not change a journey or affect privacy. Changing a contact email requires confirming the new contact before it replaces the existing one. During the change, the app explains how the person can still access their account and recover from an incorrect address.

Recovering account access returns the participant to the existing record. It does not create a fresh journey or pretend an existing email address belongs to a different participant.

A canceled or unsuccessful account action leaves the previous working state intact. The app should explain the next available action without exposing whether an unrelated person's account exists.

## Signing out

Signing out ends access on that phone; it does not end the journey or delete the account. The next person using the app must not see the previous participant's writing, progress, name, or active private pages.

After signing out, the participant returns to Sign In with public product information still available.

If changes exist only on the current phone, signing out explains the risk. The participant can remain signed in and reconnect to protect them, or explicitly choose to discard those not-yet-protected changes and sign out. The app must not silently delete them or describe them as safely available on another phone.

Detailed privacy and deletion requirements appear in Document 13. Reliable-use and interrupted-save behavior appear in Document 12.
