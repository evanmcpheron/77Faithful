## The participant's situation

A fictional participant has just started Day 1 and selected **Serve or Encourage** and **Gratitude** as the two additional practices. Their foundational practices are Read Scripture, Pray, and Reflect.

Their preferred available Bible translation is selected during setup. The same Scripture reference is assigned regardless of that translation.

## What Today communicates

# Day 1 of 77

**Week 1 · Abiding in Christ**  
**Remain in Christ**  
**Today's reading: John 15:1–11**  
**0 of 5 practices complete**

The page offers the five practice names and their current states. It also offers an optional intention invitation and a way to read the first week's introduction. Neither is an extra completion requirement.

The app should show the participant's actual journey date and time zone where relevant. This example does not invent their personal account information or starting calendar date.

## Weekly introduction — sample

The first week begins with relationship rather than performance. This journey offers a structure for reading, prayer, reflection, and action, but the structure is not the source of life with God. The invitation is to spend time with Jesus and respond to Him in the circumstances you actually have.

You may begin with enthusiasm, uncertainty, tiredness, or a mixture of all three. You do not need a perfect record before approaching God. As you read this week, notice what it means to make room for His Word, pray honestly, and depend on Him rather than your own determination alone.

Choose a realistic time to begin today. Let the daily practices support your attention to Christ, and return to Him when your day does not go as planned.

## Scripture reading

**Assigned reference:** John 15:1–11.

In the released app, the reader contains the full assigned Scripture text from the participant's selected, approved translation, together with the correct translation label and required acknowledgments. This documentation example intentionally does not reproduce that text or stand in for the finished reader.

The participant may instead read the same passage in their own Bible. The completion action remains available without proof that they opened the in-app text.

## Devotional — sample

### Remain in Christ

In John 15, Jesus calls His disciples to remain in Him. He connects this relationship with His words, obedience, love, and joy. The emphasis is not on establishing an impressive religious record, but on continuing in relationship with Him.

That distinction matters as you begin this journey. Five daily practices can give your day structure. They can help you make time to read, pray, reflect, and respond. But completing them does not earn Jesus' love, and an incomplete checklist does not mean you should stop coming to Him.

Read the passage with attention to what Jesus actually says. What does He invite His disciples to receive, remember, and practice? Consider how His teaching addresses your desire to manage growth through effort alone. Faithful action matters, but it belongs within dependence on Christ rather than replacing that dependence.

Today, begin with an honest response. You may need to ask for help making time, confess impatience, or give thanks for the opportunity to begin. Let that response shape one ordinary part of your day. The purpose of Day 1 is not to prove that you can complete 77 perfect days. It is to begin spending time with Jesus and responding to Him.

## Prayer prompt — sample

Ask Jesus to help you depend on Him rather than use this journey to prove yourself. Bring Him one situation in which you need help responding with love and obedience today.

The participant can use this direction or pray in their own words. Nothing needs to be recorded to mark Pray complete.

## Intention invitation — sample

**Where could you pause today and ask Jesus to shape your response?**

An optional written response might be: “Before a difficult conversation, I will pause and ask for help responding patiently.”

The person may leave the writing field empty or choose a different appropriate response. This intention is not a sixth practice, and the app does not require proof that the conversation occurred.

## Reflection question — sample

**Where today did you notice a need to depend on Jesus, and how did you respond?**

The person may reflect privately without writing. A sample saved entry might say:

> I was impatient during a conversation. I remembered my intention afterward rather than before it. I apologized and want to keep practicing a more attentive response tomorrow.

This is fictional example writing. It is not Scripture, an assessment of the person's spiritual condition, or a model answer the app expects everyone to imitate.

Saving the words and marking Reflect complete are separate actions.

## How the five practices might unfold

| Practice           | Example of participation                                                            | What the app records                                         |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Read Scripture     | The participant reads John 15:1–11 in a personal Bible.                             | One completion marker when they deliberately mark it.        |
| Pray               | They pray using the prompt as a starting point.                                     | One completion marker; no prayer transcript.                 |
| Reflect            | They consider the day's question and optionally save the example response.          | The completion marker and any saved writing remain separate. |
| Serve or Encourage | They send a thoughtful, appropriate message to someone who could use encouragement. | One completion marker; no recipient details or proof.        |
| Gratitude          | They intentionally thank God for a specific gift or expression of care.             | One completion marker; no required list or word count.       |

These examples are possibilities, not universal amounts or assignments. The participant is responsible for an honest declaration of their own engagement.

## What the day looks like if it stays incomplete

Suppose Scripture, prayer, and gratitude are marked complete, but the other two are not. The day shows **3 of 5 practices complete**.

When the next journey date begins, the app moves to Day 2. Day 1 remains available with its original reading, practices, and writing. The person can return to it later, but Day 2 is not blocked.

There is no failure screen, restart demand, paywall, public disclosure, or request to explain why the day was incomplete.

## Why this example matters

The Scripture provides the basis. The devotional offers explanation. Prayer invites a response to God. The intention helps connect learning with ordinary life. Reflection supports honest consideration. The two selected practices encourage concrete action.

The app's role is to guide and record that experience, not to claim that a completed set of controls proves spiritual transformation.
